The Tropical Diseases Modelling Network (TDModNet) brings together modellers, epidemiologists, statisticians, health economists, and research software engineers working on tropical infectious diseases in resource-limited settings.

The network exists because the questions facing national disease programmes rarely fit a single discipline or a single country. Deciding where to target malaria elimination effort, how to prescribe antibiotics as resistance spreads, or whether a new diagnostic is worth its cost all require combining field data, transmission models, and economic analysis — and doing it close enough to the people who make the decisions for the answers to be used.

## What we do

We build mathematical and economic models that support investigations into the transmission, control, and elimination of tropical diseases, working with partners across Southeast Asia and the wider tropics.

Our work is deliberately pragmatic. Models are calibrated to real epidemiological and programme data, developed alongside the ministries and national programmes that will use them, and released as open tools and code wherever possible.

## Research themes

Work in the network is organised around five main themes:

- **Neglected tropical diseases.** Transmission and burden modelling for regional health problems including leptospirosis, melioidosis, dengue, and tuberculosis, combined with population surveys and community-based research. This theme also looks at the factors that reshape transmission over time — ageing populations, chronic conditions such as diabetes, urbanisation, climate change, and health-seeking behaviour.
- **Antimicrobial resistance.** Analysis of resistance patterns in bacterial infections, hospital transmission and infection control, and machine-learning approaches to antibiotic prescribing. This work runs across the MORU network, including partners in Cambodia and Lao PDR.
- **Economics and implementation research.** Cost-effectiveness of diagnostics, treatments, and vaccines, extending past clinical efficacy to ask how interventions actually perform in routine care and what stands in the way of implementation.
- **Analytical tools for malaria elimination.** Transmission models calibrated to epidemiological data and historical intervention records, used with National Malaria Control Programmes to predict the impact and cost efficiency of different elimination strategies.
- **Research software engineering.** Treating software as a research output: reproducible model code, interactive applications and dashboards for programme staff, and maintained data pipelines and repositories. This theme also covers the network's use of machine learning and AI, both as research methods and as development tooling.

## Host and partners

The network is hosted by the Mathematical and Economic Modelling (MAEMOD) department of the [Mahidol Oxford Tropical Medicine Research Unit](https://www.tropmedres.ac/units/moru-bangkok/mathematical-and-economic-modelling), a research collaboration established in 1979 between Mahidol University, the University of Oxford, and the Wellcome Trust.

Network members hold appointments at Mahidol University's Faculty of Tropical Medicine and the University of Oxford's Nuffield Department of Medicine, and collaborate with national malaria and disease control programmes, ministries of health, and academic groups across the region. See [Collaboration]({{< relref "/collaboration" >}}) for partner links.

## Training and capacity building

A central aim of the network is to train new modellers and data analysts in Southeast Asia, so that regional modelling capacity is built where the diseases are.

Members contribute to Mahidol University's MSc in Biomedical and Health Informatics, teach on Oxford's MSc in International Health and Tropical Medicine, and run short courses in infectious disease modelling. The network supervises PhD, DPhil, and MSc students, and hosts placements and research attachments. Training announcements are posted under [News / Activities]({{< relref "/news" >}}).
